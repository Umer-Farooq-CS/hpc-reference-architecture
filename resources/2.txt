Here is the structured breakdown of the information from the new images provided, organized into logical architectural components:

### **Hardware & GPU Virtualization**

* The hardware layer utilizes specific node identifiers (e.g., "B1-G1-A") to map resources, where "B" indicates Bare Metal, "G" indicates the GPU number, and "A" indicates the specific Slice.
* Large Language Models (LLMs) are hosted and executed directly on this hardware layer.
* For NVIDIA infrastructure, GPU sharing and slicing are achieved through techniques including Time Slicing, MIG (Multi-Instance GPU), and vGPUs.
* NIM is also noted as a component within this ecosystem.

### **Orchestration Layer & Scheduling**

* The central Orchestration layer directly manages and provisions resources to the underlying Hardware layer.
* At the workload level, the orchestrator manages resources via "PoDs," allocating specific amounts of vCPU, RAM, and GPU through a Scheduler.
* Kubeflow is explicitly identified as an open-source tool utilized as an orchestrator.
* The underlying scheduling and orchestration stack relies on CUDA, Kubernetes (K8s), NUOP8R, and the KAI Scheduler.
* The KAI Scheduler is strategically designated to replace Run.ai.

### **Service Models**

* The orchestration layer is designed to support and feed into higher-level service offerings, which include:
* JNaaS (Jupyter Notebook as a Service)
* ModelaaS (Model as a Service)
* MLOps
* GPU PaaS (Platform as a Service)



### **Bare Metal Provisioning & Visibility**

* To achieve "LLM Visibility" and handle Bare Metal Provisioning, a specific suite of tools is utilized.
* This provisioning stack includes Slinky, Metal3, VMetal, and BCM.
